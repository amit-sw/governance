# Public research digest

Synthetic example. The request, vendor, reviewer, and human decision below are fictional.

## Request

The strategy team wants ExampleAI, an AI-enabled writing tool, to draft a weekly internal digest from its own published research notes and public documents whose licenses expressly permit this use. Two named analysts operate the tool and read its output. Nobody outside the team receives the digest, and it informs exploration rather than investment or other consequential decisions.

Inputs contain no personal, confidential, or privileged information. The tool is read-only and cannot send messages, change systems, or take actions. An analyst checks every factual claim against the linked source before circulation. Access is restricted to the two analysts. The fictional vendor receives only the permitted public text, does not train on it, and deletes working copies after seven days. The team keeps approved digests for 30 days. Required attribution is retained; source permissions cover internal summaries. Analysts monitor citation errors and can stop using the tool.

## Decision

**Approved** by a fictional human reviewer on 2026-09-03, under the illustrative starter rubric, version 0.1.

## Why

Public, permitted inputs and restricted internal use limit confidentiality and IP exposure (D1, D6). Read-only operation, source checking, and an accountable reviewer limit autonomy and reliability risk (D3, D5). The request states access, sharing, and retention controls (D2). No consequential action or sensitive-data vendor exposure triggers B1 or B2.

## Conditions

Maintain the public-only input boundary and source checking. Revisit the proposal before adding confidential data, external circulation, or action-taking capabilities.
