# Autonomous hiring screening

Synthetic example. The request, vendor, reviewer, and human decision below are fictional.

## Request

Recruiting proposes a HirePilot AI agent to rank applications and automatically reject applicants below a threshold. Named recruiters operate the dashboard; applicants receive rejection emails directly from the agent. Company-held resumes and candidate answers include personal employment and education information. The stated applicant permissions cover recruiting use. Sensitive health and demographic attributes are excluded from the intended inputs.

The fictional vendor is expressly approved for these candidate inputs, does not train on them, and deletes its processing copies after 30 days. Recruiters have role-limited access, and the company retains recruiting records for the stated six-month pilot. The agent writes outcomes into the hiring system and sends rejection emails without prior human approval. A recruiter samples ten percent of decisions afterward and can stop future runs, but rejected applicants may have already lost an opportunity. The company owns the job descriptions and has stated permission for the resume processing. There is no validated method for checking whether the rankings are reliable or fair.

## Decision

**Rejected in the proposed form** by a fictional human reviewer on 2026-09-15, under the illustrative starter rubric, version 0.1.

## Why

B2 applies to consequential employment decisions executed without prior human approval. Autonomy and impact are high (D3, D4), and unvalidated rankings used for consequential decisions create high reliability risk (D5). Suitable vendor handling does not dilute these dimensions. Review after rejection does not provide the required approval point.

## Conditions for reconsideration

Remove automated rejection and sending permissions. Use outputs only as human-reviewed drafts or recommendations, define validation and escalation procedures, and obtain appropriate specialist review before reconsidering the use.
