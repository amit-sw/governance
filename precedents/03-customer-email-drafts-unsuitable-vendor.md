# Customer email drafts using an unsuitable vendor

Synthetic example. The request, vendor, reviewer, and human decision below are fictional.

## Request

The customer support team wants the same DraftDesk product to draft replies from company-owned service tickets. Support staff would review every draft before sending it to customers. The tool would not issue refunds or change customer accounts. Tickets contain names, email addresses, order references, and private customer conversations.

In this proposal, staff would use shared public accounts rather than the enterprise configuration. The fictional vendor is explicitly unapproved for personal and confidential ticket data, and its public-account terms explicitly allow training on uploads without the required customer permission. Upload access is unrestricted within the support team. There is no deletion control over vendor training copies. Local drafts would be kept for 30 days. The company owns the support templates but lacks permission for vendor training on customer conversations. A supervisor would sample sent replies weekly.

## Decision

**Rejected** by a fictional human reviewer on 2026-09-12, under the illustrative starter rubric, version 0.1.

## Why

B1 applies because personal and confidential inputs go to a vendor explicitly unsuitable for those inputs. Sharing and retention exposure are high (D1, D2). Human review of outgoing messages does not remove the earlier disclosure or training problem. Limited output autonomy does not offset that blocker.

## Conditions for reconsideration

Use an expressly suitable configuration with permission, restricted accounts, no-training controls, and defined deletion, or redesign the proposal to use entirely synthetic inputs.

## Relationship to the other email precedent

The approved-with-conditions email example has the same business purpose and product name but materially different data-handling controls. These fictional outcomes illustrate why tool similarity or an earlier approval does not determine a new recommendation.
